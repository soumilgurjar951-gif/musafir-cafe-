Our story — page one

A wooden deck,
a kettle, and time.

In 1998, Dorje — a bus driver who kept missing his own halts — parked an old tea stall under the deodars near Char Dukan and never left.

The stall grew a roof, then a deck, then a row of mismatched wooden benches that have held twenty-seven years of travellers. Trekkers heading for Dhanaulti, writers hiding from deadlines, couples who took a wrong turn on Mall Road and never corrected it — everyone finds the same thing here: a hot kulhad pressed into cold hands, and nobody in a hurry.

We still brew on a slow wood stove. The water boils differently at this height, and so do conversations. There is no music playlist — only wind, kettles, and the occasional story someone finally tells out loud. In winter, the famous Mussoorie winterline burns red over the Doon valley — the whole deck goes quiet for it.
